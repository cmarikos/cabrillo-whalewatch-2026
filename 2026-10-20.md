#### Earth's Oceans
- 71% covered by water, 97% oceans ~ 2.5% fresh water, mostly 68% locked in ice, 30% groundwater, 0.3% surface water
- Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic, Southern 
##### Dimensions
- Surface Area 139 million square miles

##### Tides
- Frequency - two high tides and two low tides per day
- "[[King Tides]]" - sun and moon are closely lined up, increasing the gravitational power of the tides
- Tides are more extreme at Earth's poles
##### Currents
- Wind, tides, changes in water density, and rotation of the earth
- Surface currents - top 10%
	- Wind and tides by the shore
	- Wind is the driving force in the open ocean 
	- Water as deep at 400m is still affected by wind
	- Big loops called [[gyres]] clockwise in Northern Hemisphere, counter clockwise in the Southern Hemisphere because of the [[Coriolis effect]]
- Deep ocean - other lower 90%
	- Driven by changes in the density of water
	- Temperature and salinity affect density
	- [[Thermohaline Circulation]] -- forms the [[Global Conveyor Belt]]
		- Rising sea temps are causing the GCB to slow down
		- The GCB moved very slowly

#### Southern California Bight

![[Screenshot 2026-10-20 at 7.45.23 PM.png]]

About 30% of all cetacean species can be found in the [[Southern California Bight]] because of the water temps and upwelling

##### Upwelling
- colder, more nutrient rich water is pulled up when surface winds push water away from an area (typically in coastal areas)
- Whales tend to be found in upwelling areas
- This is because the nutrients feed the food chain the whole way up beginning at [[phytoplankton]] --> [[zooplankton]] --> small fish --> bigger fish and mammals
- When animals die, they sink to the bottom and the nutrients sink to continue the cycle

##### [[El Niño]]
- Natural Event 2-7 years
- Normal year - equatorial winds gather toward the west, cold waters around South America
- El Niňo year - the winds weaken, and warm water currents flow back to South America, warmer winter in North America, much drier season in Asia
- Evidence that El Niňos are more intense and the cycles are more frequent
- El Niňo can drive habitat loss 
	- In [[California Kelp Forests]] - Kelp like colder water, and the warmer water destroys them - this also has ripples up the food chain
		- This has also led to the explosion in sea urchin populations whose predators, the Sunflower Sea Stars aren't eating them because the water is too warm and there was a blight, the purple sea urchins eat the kelp, and too many of them eating kelp destroys the kelp forests - and the sea urchins are in a starvation state because they've grown beyond carrying capacity
	- Because waters are warmer, species travel outside of their typical ranges, much further north
	- Abnormally high rainfall
	- [[Demoic Acid]] - algae blooms also kill sea life - the Palisades fires run off caused a large build up of demoic acid on that coast
